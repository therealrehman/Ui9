Privacy Policy

Ok Launcher by Ok Labs · Effective date: October 20, 2026

In short: Ok Launcher does not collect, store on any server, share or sell your personal data. The app has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no internet permission. Everything it knows stays on your phone.

The same text is inside the app (Settings > Privacy policy) and works without internet.

1. Who we are

Ok Launcher (“the app”) is an Android home-screen launcher made by Ok Labs (“we”, “us”), a company that builds Android apps. Contact: a.rehmanmazher11@gmail.com.

2. Data we collect

None. We do not collect any personal information, device identifiers, location, contacts, messages, photos or usage statistics. The app does not request the Android INTERNET permission, so it cannot send data off your phone. We run no servers for the app.

3. Data kept on your phone

To work, the app saves the following in its private storage on your phone only:

  • your Home screen layout (which apps, folders and widgets are on which page, the dock);
  • your settings (grid size, icon size, themes, hidden apps, Edge panel apps, widget style and similar choices);
  • a small in-memory cache of app icons, which is cleared when the app closes.
  • a short crash note, only if the app crashes (the error, app and Android version, phone model). It is shown in Settings so you can email it to us if you want; the app never sends it.

This data never leaves your phone through the app. It is deleted when you uninstall the app or clear its storage in Android settings.

Backups. The “Back up layout and settings” feature writes a text file to a place you choose (for example Downloads or a cloud drive). That file is created and held by you; we never receive it. Separately, Android’s own system backup may include the app’s saved settings in your Google account backup, depending on your phone’s settings. That is handled by Android and Google, not by us.

4. Permissions and what they are used for

Permission / accessWhy the app needs it
Seeing installed apps (queries for launchable apps)To show your apps on the Home screen and the Apps screen, and to find the default Phone, Messages, Browser, Camera and Settings apps. The list is used on the phone only.
Notification access (optional)Only if you turn on notification badges or “Notifications in Quick panel”. For badges the app counts how many notifications each app has to draw a dot or a number on its icon. For the Quick panel it shows the app name, title and text of the notifications on your screen, with swipe to remove and tap to open, and what is playing (title, artist, cover) with previous / play / next buttons. This is held in memory only while the notifications are showing: it is never saved, shared or sent anywhere (the app has no internet access). Ongoing notifications and group summaries are not counted or listed. You can turn the Quick panel list off in Settings > Gestures, and the access at any time in Android Settings.
Accessibility service (optional)Only if you turn on “Open in every app” in Settings > Quick panel. The service listens only for “a window changed” events and places an invisible bar over the status bar to catch a pull-down, then shows this app’s Quick panel instead of the phone’s own panel. It takes a picture of the screen only to blur it behind the panel; the picture stays in memory for a moment and is never saved or sent. It cannot read the text or content of other apps, and nothing is saved, shared or sent anywhere. Switch it off in Android Settings > Accessibility.
Device administrator, “lock the screen” only (optional)Only if you turn on “Double-tap to lock”. It lets the app lock your screen when you double-tap an empty spot. It cannot wipe data, change passwords or read anything. You can switch it off in Settings > Security > Device admin apps.
Expand status barTo open the notification panel when you swipe down on the Home screen.
Request to delete packagesTo start Android’s own “Uninstall app?” screen when you choose Uninstall in the quick menu. Android always asks you to confirm.
Set wallpaper hintsTo let the wallpaper slide a little when you swipe between pages.
Set wallpaperOnly when you tap “Set as wallpaper” in Settings > Wallpaper: the app draws the gradient you picked and gives it to Android as your wallpaper. Nothing is read from your photos.
Wi-Fi, network and Bluetooth stateOnly to show on the Quick panel whether Wi-Fi, mobile data and Bluetooth are on or off. The app cannot switch them and never reads network names, nearby devices or your location.
Modify system settingsOnly when you move the brightness slider or tap Auto rotate on the Quick panel. Android asks you to allow it once in its own screen. The app changes nothing else.
Do not disturb accessOnly when you tap Do not disturb or Mute on the Quick panel. Android asks you to allow it once in its own screen. The app does not read your notifications for this.
WidgetsThe app can host widgets from other apps and has its own clock and date widgets. Widgets from other apps are drawn by those apps and follow their own privacy policies.

5. Sharing

We do not share, sell or rent any data, because we do not have any. The app contains no advertising or analytics code and no third-party libraries that collect data.

6. Contacting us

In the app, Settings > Contact us has “Email us”, “Report a problem” and “Privacy question or request”. They open your own email app with a message to a.rehmanmazher11@gmail.com. You can read and change the message before you send it, and nothing is sent unless you press Send. “Report a problem” fills in the app version, the Android version and the phone model to help us find the bug; you can delete them. We use what you send only to answer you and to fix the app.

7. Other apps and links

When you open an app from the launcher, or a link such as this policy, that app or your browser takes over and its own privacy practices apply.

8. Children

The app is a general-purpose tool and is not directed at children under 13. Because it collects no personal data, it does not knowingly collect any from children.

9. Security

Your layout and settings are kept in the app’s private storage, which other apps cannot read on an unmodified Android phone. Nothing is transmitted by the app.

10. Your rights and choices

Because we hold no personal data about you, there is nothing for us to access, correct or delete. You control everything the app stores: clear it in Android Settings > Apps > Ok Launcher > Storage, or uninstall the app. If you have a privacy question or request, email a.rehmanmazher11@gmail.com.

11. Changes to this policy

If the app changes in a way that affects privacy (for example, if a future version ever adds internet features), we will update this page and the effective date above before the change is released.
